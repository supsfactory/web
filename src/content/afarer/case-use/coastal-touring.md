Coastal touring and exploration demand boards that track straight, glide efficiently between strokes, and handle moderate chop. The displacement hull design of the SUP Tour 12' cuts through water for efficient long-distance paddling, and touring paddlers typically cover 5–15 km per session, exploring coastlines, navigating between beaches, and accessing remote coves.

## What makes a touring board different

A touring SUP is a compromise tuned for distance: a longer hull than an all-around board for glide, a nose that parts chop instead of slapping it, and a shape that holds a heading without constant correction. Touring boards run from 11'6" for shorter expeditions up to 14' for endurance paddling, and their extra length translates directly into speed — roughly one knot per session for every additional foot of waterline.

## Matching the board to the paddler

Choosing between a 12-foot and 14-foot touring board comes down to the paddler's weight, fitness and goals. A 12' touring board suits intermediate paddlers covering coastal distances in moderate conditions, while the 14' length rewards fit paddlers chasing kilometres and holding pace into a headwind. Both should carry a leash that matches the environment — coiled for open water — and sufficient flotation for the paddler's weight with gear aboard.

## The displacement advantage

Where an all-around board rides over the surface, a displacement nose parts the water, which reduces drag and keeps speed through a gentle chop. That is the difference between a morning paddle that feels like work and one that feels like gliding. For tours that mix open water and sheltered sections, the same hull that tracks straight in a headwind still turns responsively in confined channels and harbours.

## Recommended touring setups

For day tours and exploration we recommend the SUP Tour 12' with a touring paddle — a larger blade with an offset angle for efficient long strokes — plus a deck bag for food, phone and navigation. For multi-day expeditions, pair the board with the Kayak Lite system to convert it for seated paddling, extending range and comfort on long crossings.

Touring is where inflatable boards genuinely match rigid performance: the extra pressure rating of a touring board delivers the stiffness needed for efficient glide, and packability means the board travels in the car or on the boat instead of living on a roof rack.
