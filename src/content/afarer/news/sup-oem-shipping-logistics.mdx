### The Direct Answer

Your SUP order is quoted on one of three incoterms: **FOB Qingdao**, **CIF** or **DDP**. FOB means the factory's responsibility ends at the port of loading — you take over freight, insurance and import customs at the destination. CIF adds the main ocean freight and insurance to the factory's side, up to the destination port. DDP puts everything on the factory — sea freight, import duties and delivery to your warehouse — and you receive goods at a single, total landed price. Production takes 30–45 days after PO and deposit; sea freight adds 4–6 weeks to the EU or US. The right term depends on your experience with imports: sophisticated importers choose FOB for control and better freight rates; first-time brands usually prefer DDP because the price they see is the price they pay.

### The Shipping Timeline After Production

- **Week 0** — bank-deposit cleared against the deposit payment, production starts.
- **Weeks 4–6** — production complete, 100-point QC and 48-hour pressure hold passed, export packing and container loading.
- **Weeks 5–8** — ocean freight in transit to the EU/US, with shipping documents (bill of lading, invoice, packing list, compliance documents) sent ahead of arrival.
- **Week 8–10** — arrival and customs clearance at destination, then delivery to your warehouse or distribution point.

That is why launch planning should always add freight on top of production: a board finished in 45 days still needs 4–6 weeks at sea. Air freight exists for urgent waves, but it is priced in tens of dollars per kilogram — a container of boards is the economic route.

### What Each Incoterm Covers

**FOB Qingdao (Free On Board)**
- Factory covers: production, export packing, customs clearance in China, loading onto the vessel.
- You cover: ocean freight, marine insurance, import duties and destination customs, delivery from the arrival port.
- Best for: importers with freight accounts or forwarder relationships, and anyone wanting to control the logistics spend.

**CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight)**
- Factory covers: production, export packing, clearance, ocean freight and insurance to the destination port.
- You cover: import duties, destination customs and delivery from port to your warehouse.
- Best for: buyers who want the freight handled by the factory but keep import handling themselves.

**DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)**
- Factory covers: everything — production, export, ocean freight, insurance, import duties, destination customs and delivery to your warehouse.
- You cover: payment of the single quoted price.
- Best for: first-time importers, brands without forwarders, and projects where a stable total landed cost matters more than freight savings. This is the most popular choice for new SUP brands.

### Container Math and Packing

Inflatable SUP is a high-value-per-cubic-meter product: a 20-foot container holds a substantial branded order, and a 40-foot container holds roughly double — hundreds of boards plus paddle, leash, bag, pump and packaging per unit. The factory consolidates accessories and boards into export cartons per your packaging spec, and full-container or shared-container (LCL) options exist for smaller runs. A good factory confirms the exact load plan with you before production starts, so your MOQ matches a real container budget rather than a back-of-envelope guess.

### Import Duties and Documents — What to Prepare

Destination-side costs vary by market, so confirm these before signing the PO:

- **EU** — import duty on inflatable paddle boards plus VAT; CE, REACH and RoHS documents must accompany the shipment.
- **US** — customs entry with the correct product code for "inflatable paddle boards," USCG paperwork where applicable, and an appraised value consistent with your invoice.
- **Everywhere** — packing list, commercial invoice, bill of lading and, with SUPsfactory, the compliance document set described in [CE Certification for Inflatable SUP — What Buyers Must Check](/news/ce-certification-inflatable-sup).

Under FOB and CIF these are your responsibility with the help of your customs broker; under DDP the factory's logistics team handles them — which is why DDP buyers should share their target warehouse address, product description and any supplier-declaration requirements at quotation stage.

### Choosing the Right Term for Your Business

- **You have imported before** — go FOB and use your own forwarder; you can match or beat the factory's freight rates and keep control of the schedule.
- **You want one predictable price** — go DDP. The quote you approve is the total cost delivered to your door; nobody can surprise you with a freight or duty bill later.
- **Your bank or partner requires supplier-inclusive terms** — CIF balances factory responsibility with your own import handling.

Whatever the term, the factory's responsibilities never change: export packing, full container-loading, shipping documents ahead of the vessel, and compliance paperwork in the consignment. The incoterm changes who pays the destination side — not the quality of the box.

### FAQ

**What is the difference between FOB and DDP for a first SUP order?** FOB hands you freight, insurance and import handling after the port of loading; DDP includes everything through delivery to your warehouse. First-time brand owners almost always find DDP simpler — one price, no import logistics.

**How long does sea freight from Qingdao take?** 4–6 weeks to the EU and US on standard ocean routes, after production completes (30–45 days from PO and deposit).

**Can I use air freight for a small order?** Yes, and trial orders sometimes ship by air. Expect pricing in tens of dollars per kilogram — fine for samples, uneconomic at container scale.

**Who pays import duties under CIF?** You do — CIF covers freight and insurance to the destination port; import duties, destination customs and inland delivery are outside the price.

**Do I need a customs broker for DDP?** No — under DDP the factory's logistics team clears the goods and delivers them to your warehouse; you only prepare the product information the factory requests.

**What documents ship with the container?** Packing list, commercial invoice and bill of lading, plus the compliance set (CE, REACH, RoHS) — and the factory sends the documents ahead of the vessel so clearance can start early.
