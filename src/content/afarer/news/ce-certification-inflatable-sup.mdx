### The Direct Answer

CE marking is the mandatory EU health-and-safety marking for inflatable SUP — no CE mark, no legal sale in the EU. But a certificate is only as good as what it covers: you need a factory that can show you (1) a CE certificate naming your exact model, (2) the underlying test reports, (3) the product classification that drives testing, and (4) the supporting documentation set — REACH and RoHS declarations, plus USCG approval when shipping to the US. Buyers who only ask "are you CE certified?" get a yes; buyers who ask for the report get proof. A reputable SUP factory supplies full compliance documentation with every shipment, and certificate numbers are available on request.

### How an Inflatable SUP Gets Classified

EU compliance starts with classification, because the classification decides which standards and testing apply:

- **Personal flotation devices** (PPE category) — the strictest route, used for rescue-type or life-jacket-style products.
- **Water sports / buoyancy aids** — the typical classification for recreational paddle boards, tested for buoyancy, load capacity and stability.
- **Toys** — small, child-oriented boards can fall under the Toy Safety Directive instead.

Inflatable SUPs are usually tested as buoyancy aids or comparable recreational craft (ISO 6185-1 for inflatable boats is a common reference for construction and pressure testing). The board's geometry, intended use and documentation decide the route — which is exactly why the factory's engineer should classify your product at quotation stage, not after production.

### What the Buyers' Pack Must Contain

Ask for these five documents before placing an order:

1. **CE certificate** — names the model, the manufacturer and the applicable standards, and carries the certificate number and issue date.
2. **Test reports** — the actual laboratory results behind the certificate (pressure, buoyancy, load, seam strength, material tests). A certificate without reports is a claim, not proof.
3. **REACH and RoHS declarations** — chemical-compliance documents for the materials in the board, cover, pad and accessories, required for EU sale and increasingly demanded by US and UK distributors.
4. **USCG approval** (for US markets) — US Coast Guard type approval or the relevant import paperwork for the US market.
5. **Factory certifications** — ISO 9001 for quality management and BSCI for social compliance show the certificate is backed by a real, audited plant.

### How to Verify a Certificate Is Valid for Your Model

- **Model name must match.** A certificate for "inflatable paddle boards" that does not name your model number is a marketing document, not coverage. Ask for the certificate for the exact SKU you are buying.
- **Check the dates.** Certificates expire and tests get re-issued. Ask when the report was issued and whether the current production version matches the tested one — material or construction changes after testing can invalidate coverage.
- **Confirm the classification.** The same board can be certified as a toy in one market and a buoyancy aid in another. Your target market decides which document set you need.
- **Ask for the number.** A factory that publishes its certificate numbers and offers them on request (as SUPsfactory does) is far likelier to have them than one that describes its "certification system" without specifics.

### Common Pitfalls in SUP Compliance

- **"CE" stamped without a document trail** — a mark on the board without a matching certificate is a legal risk for the brand, not the factory.
- **Certificates that cover the factory, not the product** — ISO 9001 proves process quality; it is not CE. Both are needed, and they are different documents.
- **Artwork changes that invalidate testing** — graphics do not affect safety testing, but construction changes (thicker PVC, new valve, changed seams) do. Lock construction before testing, not after.
- **No country-by-country plan** — EU needs CE plus REACH/RoHS; the US needs USCG paperwork; other markets have their own import requirements. ### FAQ

**Is CE certification required for SUP sold in the EU?** Yes — CE marking is mandatory for legally selling inflatable SUP in the EU market, alongside REACH and RoHS chemical compliance.

**Does the US require CE certification?** No — the US uses its own system (USCG approval and consumer-product rules such as CPSIA where applicable), but CE plus REACH/RoHS documentation is still required by many US distributors and big retailers.

**Can any factory issue a CE certificate?** No — CE testing is carried out by accredited laboratories and, for higher-risk products, a notified body must be involved. The factory supplies the certificate; the testing is independent.

**What if my board's artwork changes after certification?** Graphics and colors generally do not change the safety-test status, but construction changes (material grade, seams, valves) may require re-testing. Confirm with the factory before changing construction.

**Do sample orders come with compliance documents?** Trial and sample orders ship with the relevant documentation for their market; full production orders ship with the complete document set.
