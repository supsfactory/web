### Two Paths to Market

When launching an inflatable SUP brand, one of the first decisions is whether to use a private label or OEM manufacturing approach. Both have advantages, and the right choice depends on your budget, timeline, brand ambitions, and risk tolerance.

### Private Label Manufacturing

Private label means selecting existing board designs from a manufacturer's catalog and adding your brand's graphics, logo, and packaging.

**Best for:** First-time brand owners, small-batch launches, and businesses testing a new market.

### OEM Manufacturing

OEM (original equipment manufacturing) means the manufacturer builds boards to your specifications — custom dimensions, shape, materials, and performance characteristics.

**Best for:** Established brands, unique product concepts, and businesses building long-term brand equity.

### Decision Framework

**Choose private label if:**

- Your budget is under $20,000 for your first inventory order
- You need to be in market within 8 weeks
- You want to validate demand before investing in custom tooling
- You are a retailer, rental operator, or distributor adding a house brand

**Choose OEM if:**

- You have a unique board design or performance requirement
- You plan to build a long-term brand with proprietary products
- Your budget allows for mold investment ($3K–$10K+ per mold)
- You need exclusive rights to your product design
- You have a minimum first order quantity of 200+ units

### The Hybrid Approach: Private Label First, Then OEM

Many successful SUP brands start with private label to establish distribution and validate their market, then transition to OEM as volumes grow:

1. **Year 1**: Launch 1–2 private label models. Build brand awareness, retail relationships, and online presence.
2. **Year 2–3**: Introduce 1 OEM flagship model. Use customer feedback to design a board that fills a gap in your line.
3. **Year 3+**: Expand OEM lineup as private label models phase out. Your brand becomes known for proprietary designs.
