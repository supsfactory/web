### The Direct Answer

Private-label SUP means selling inflatable boards manufactured by a factory under your own brand — the factory builds, you own the market. The process runs in six steps: **(1)** choose a factory you can verify, **(2)** send your specification, **(3)** approve samples, **(4)** place the PO at the right MOQ tier, **(5)** receive compliance documents, and **(6)** take delivery on your shipping terms. The whole journey — from first specification to a delivered container — typically takes 12–16 weeks: about 1 week for engineering review and quotation, 2–3 weeks for sampling, 30–45 days of production, and 4–6 weeks of sea freight. This guide walks every step and links to the deep-dive article for each one.

### Step 1: Choose a Factory You Can Verify

The factory choice decides everything downstream — pricing, quality, compliance and delivery. Verify four things before you talk details: the plant (factory photos, capacity, in-house engineering), the quality system (inspection checklist, pressure testing), the certifications (CE, ISO 9001, BSCI, REACH, RoHS — certificates on request) and the company structure (who you are actually contracting with). A factory that is also a trading company, or that resells competitors' products, should be a red flag. Read the full checklist in [How to Choose a Reliable SUP OEM Manufacturer](/news/how-to-choose-sup-oem-manufacturer).

### Step 2: Send Your Specification

Eight items turn your idea into a quotable project: board geometry and weight target, construction targets, materials and accessories, print-ready artwork, packaging and labels, volume and MOQ, target-market compliance, and budget with timeline. You do not need engineering documents — a reference board, a sketch or even a competitor's link is enough to start. With complete information, the factory returns an engineering review and quotation within one business day. Details in [What Information Does a Brand Need Before Starting SUP Production](/news/info-needed-before-sup-production).

### Step 3: Approve Samples

Expect the first physical sample in 15–20 days. Use the sample round to validate the board itself and the artwork on real PVC — screen colors do not survive PVC printing without a Pantone reference or printed proof. Lock the construction at this stage: changes after sample approval (thicker PVC, new valve, different seams) restart engineering and may re-trigger compliance testing. The full sampling logic sits inside [How Long Does It Take To Develop A Custom SUP Product](/news/custom-sup-development-timeline).

### Step 4: Place the PO at the Right MOQ Tier

Your volume picks the tier: 5–10 pcs for a trial run, 50 pcs per design for a standard OEM batch, 200 pcs for a custom mould or full private-label project. Unit price is driven by volume, construction grade, tooling, accessories, print complexity and packaging — not by a price list. Trial runs first is the safest entry: real production quality, real QC data, small money. The complete pricing model is in [How SUP OEM MOQ and Pricing Work](/news/sup-oem-moq-pricing).

### Step 5: Receive Compliance Documents

CE, REACH, RoHS and, for the US, USCG documentation must travel with the goods. Verify that the certificate names your exact model, that reports back the certificate, and that the classification matches your target market — a certificate for "inflatable paddle boards" that does not name your SKU is not coverage. Ask for the certificate number at quotation stage, not after production. How to check the paperwork properly: [CE Certification for Inflatable SUP — What Buyers Must Check](/news/ce-certification-inflatable-sup).

### Step 6: Take Delivery on Your Terms

Production completes 30–45 days after PO and deposit; sea freight adds 4–6 weeks to the EU or US. Choose the incoterm that matches your experience: FOB Qingdao if you import regularly with your own forwarder, CIF to split freight and destination handling, DDP for one predictable total price delivered to your warehouse — the most popular choice for new brands. Shipping documents (packing list, invoice, bill of lading) travel ahead of the vessel. Compare the terms in [SUP Shipping from China: FOB vs CIF vs DDP](/news/sup-oem-shipping-logistics).

### The 12–16 Week Timeline at a Glance

- **Week 1** — specification submitted, engineering review and quotation within one business day, PO signed.
- **Weeks 2–4** — sample production; sample approval ends the round.
- **Weeks 5–10** — mass production (30–45 days) with QC and pressure testing.
- **Weeks 11–14** — sea freight to the EU/US with documents ahead of the vessel.
- **Week 15–16** — customs clearance and delivery to your warehouse.

Timeline, pricing, compliance and logistics are the four questions every new brand asks — the guides above answer each one in depth, and [Custom SUP Product Development — From Concept to Container](/product-development) walks the engineering side of the same journey. When you are ready, the factory will quote your project within one business day.

### FAQ

**Do I need my own designs for private label?** No — you can start from a proven platform with your logo, colors and packaging (ODM route), or develop your own board shape (OEM route). Both end with your brand on the product.

**How much capital do I need to start?** The entry point is the trial tier at 5–10 pcs; the first real order is typically the 50-pc standard batch. Production starts against a deposit, so your cash exposure is staged: deposit → balance before shipment.

**Who handles customs?** Under DDP the factory does — duties and delivery included in one price. Under FOB or CIF, you or your customs broker handle destination-side costs and clearance.

**What if the sample does not match my expectations?** That is exactly what the sample round is for: you request revisions before production starts. Most sample corrections are artwork or accessory changes; construction changes may add tooling or re-testing.

**Can I sell in both the EU and the US with one production run?** Yes — but tell the factory both markets before production so the document set covers CE, REACH, RoHS and USCG requirements in one shipment.
