### What "Reliable" Means for a SUP OEM Factory

A reliable OEM manufacturer is one that can be verified — you can check its production floor, its quality records and its certification documents — and one that behaves predictably: clear specifications, disciplined sampling, written commitments and no shortcuts on confidentiality. Everything below is what we would check if we were in your position.

### 1. Verify It Is Actually a Factory

The single biggest risk in SUP sourcing is not a bad factory — it is a trading company presenting itself as one. Red flags include: no floor photos or equipment lists, no willingness to schedule a video walkthrough, "minimum order quantities" that make no sense for a production plant, and catalogs that never name a specific production line.

What a real factory can show you: a named manufacturing plant (with an address you can look up), equipment like RF welding machines and CNC drop-stitch cutting tables, a production capacity, and a floor you can tour on video before committing. We run a 12,000 m² plant in Qingdao with 200+ employees and a dedicated SUP production line — that kind of statement should be verifiable, not decorative.

### 2. Assess the Depth of Quality Control

Anyone can claim "strict QC." What matters is where the checks sit in the process:

- **Incoming material QC** — fabric rolls, drop-stitch material and valves checked before they enter the line.
- **In-process checkpoints** — seam strength and valve sealing verified during assembly, not after.
- **Pressure hold test** — every board inflated and held at pressure for a defined period before packing.
- **Final inspection** — visual, dimensional and inflation checks against the approved sample.
- **Batch traceability** — each batch tied to a production record you can request.

Ask for a sample QC record and a test-report template. A real factory has both; a trader does not.

### 3. Verify Certifications Are Real

ISO 9001, CE and BSCI are table stakes for an export-focused SUP factory, but certificates are also the easiest documents to counterfeit. Verify three things: the certificate number, the certifying body, and the scope of the certificate (it must cover inflatable water sports equipment manufacturing, not just "trading").

Also ask when the last factory audit happened and whether you may see the audit summary. In the EU market, your CE technical file links back to the manufacturer's production documentation — a gap here becomes your problem at border control.

### 4. Judge the Factory by Its Sample Behavior

How a factory handles sampling tells you more than its website. Watch for:

- Does it push back with engineering questions (load capacity, target weight, drop-stitch thickness) instead of just saying yes?
- Does sampling take 15–20 days as promised, with a documented sample-approval sheet?
- Does the production sample match the pre-production sample?

A factory that treats your sample as a contract — documented, measured, approved — is a factory you can scale with.

### 5. Check How It Handles Intellectual Property

Your designs, graphics and tooling are the most valuable thing you will hand over. A reliable partner signs an NDA before any file exchange and states in writing that your artwork, tooling and specification files remain your property and are never reused, resold or shown to another client. Walk away from any factory that treats confidentiality as negotiable.

### 6. Ask These 10 Questions

1. Can we verify your factory address, floor photos and equipment list?
2. How many SUP boards do you produce annually, and who audits your QC?
3. Can you share a blank QC record and a pressure-test template?
4. What is your sample lead time, and what does a sample cost?
5. What is your production lead time after PO and deposit?
6. Which certifications do you hold, with certificate numbers?
7. Do you sign an NDA before receiving design files?
8. Are the pressure ratings on your boards tested and documented per batch?
9. What trade terms do you support (FOB, CIF, DDP)?
10. Can we schedule an on-site or video audit during production?

### Red Flags Summary

No production address, no QC paperwork, certificates you cannot verify, sample timelines that slip without explanation, and pressure to pay a large deposit before seeing a sample — any one of these is enough to keep looking. The right factory earns your order by being boringly verifiable, step by step.
